What you want from the people you write to

Each email is written for one person from what their business does. In inquiry mode you are the customer or partner: a financial advisor is asked about taking you on, a builder about a job, a studio about working together. In offer mode you have something to propose to them.

Be concrete: what, roughly when, where if it matters, and what you would need from them. This sentence becomes the point of every email. "I want to work with you" produces vague emails; "quotes for a 40 sqm kitchen extension in Lekki, work to start in March" produces good ones.

How the emails are written

Either way, every contact is first sorted by the sector their company works in (construction, financial advisory, banking, HR, …) and by who they are (executive, manager, specialist, admin or shared inbox), and the message is shaped for both: an owner gets a short ask for a decision, an assistant gets a two-line request to pass it on.

Write it as you would to one person. {{ '{{first_name}}' }} becomes their first name (or "there" when it is unknown). If you leave the greeting out, one is added. Plain words about the actual job pass spam filters; marketing words, capitals, links and exclamation marks do not, and the page warns you when it sees them. Your signature is added underneath.

Sending

With no limit, your mailbox's own ceiling applies: Google allows 500 a day on free Gmail and 2,000 on Workspace. Cold email near those numbers gets an account flagged, so a new mailbox is safer around 30–50.

When someone replies

Replies are read and answered automatically (or wait in the Outbox if approval is on). Tell the assistant how you want them handled; it already never argues with a “no” and never answers legal or angry messages.

Email signature

Goes at the bottom of every email and reply. Plain text or HTML: links (<a href="…">), line breaks (<br>), bold. Put {{ '{{logo}}' }} where the image should appear, or leave it out and the image goes last. Keep it short — a long signature with many links reads as marketing.
