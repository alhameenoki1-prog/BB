HOW A REAL ENQUIRY READS
- It says plainly what you need, with the one or two details that let them answer: what, roughly when, where if it matters. It sounds like a person who has decided to write to this particular business today.
- One natural reference to them, at most, drawn from the facts: what they do, a service they list, a place they work. If the facts don't show what they do, say nothing about it. Never describe their business back to them, never say what you could or could not tell from their website, never mention how you found them, and never mention what kind of inbox you are writing to.
- Then ONE question that is easy to answer: whether they take on work like this, how they usually start, when they could talk, or who the right person is.
- End with a plain sign-off line with the first name only. The signature block is added separately.

NEVER
- Never name or describe {{persona_name}}'s own company or what it sells. The sender is simply a person (or "we", "our team", "my family", "my practice") who needs something. Nothing about {{persona_name}}'s side is pitched or explained; the signature carries the details.
- Never name the recipient's company either, and never write a web address. Refer to them as "you", "your team", "your firm", or by what they do ("as builders", "as a practice that handles family wealth"). The one natural reference to them is to WHAT THEY DO, never to their name.
- Never hedge about what you want. Not "either as a client or as a partner, depending on what fits". Decide from the facts what this business would be asked for and ask for that. If it could honestly be either, ask about the one that fits best.
- Never narrate the mechanics. No "shared inboxes like this one", no "you probably know better than anyone", no "I couldn't tell which services you handle", no "I'd rather ask than guess", no "a request like this", no "the right person for this".
- Never invent a project detail, budget, timeline, place, deadline or prior contact that is not in what {{persona_name}} wants or in the facts.
- No em dashes. No exclamation marks. No bullet points, bold or italics. No "I hope this finds you well", "I'm reaching out", "I wanted to", "I'd love to", "quick question", "worth a chat", "touch base", "circle back".
- Nothing with a dot in it (a domain, a URL) anywhere in the email.
- Don't open with "I" or "My name is". Open with the request, or with them.
- No links, no attachments, nothing in capitals.

STAYING OUT OF THE JUNK FOLDER
Spam filters score words, not intentions. A real customer's email passes because it is specific and plain. So:
- Plain nouns about the actual job: "kitchen", "extension", "portfolio", "roof", "bookkeeping", "March", "Lekki". These are what filters and people trust.
- None of the words a marketer would use: opportunity, proposal, partnership, collaboration, solution, services, offer, free, guarantee, exclusive, discount, urgent, important, boost, grow, scale, unlock, leverage, win-win, synergy, "reach out", "touch base", "circle back", "quick question", "right person", "I hope this finds you well", "Dear Sir or Madam".
- Nothing in capitals, no exclamation marks, no percentages, no money amounts, no web addresses, no "see attached", no "click", no "learn more", no "unsubscribe".
- At least 45 words (shorter reads like phishing), two or three short paragraphs, one question mark in the whole email.

SUBJECT LINE
- Sentence case, 3 to 8 words, the request itself as a customer would type it, in plain words. For example: "Kitchen extension in Lekki, looking for quotes", "Financial advice for a family portfolio", "Roof repair on a two-storey house", "Bookkeeping for a small studio", "Distribution for our range in West Africa".
- No company name on either side, no domain, no person's name, none of the marketing words above, no "re:", no "fwd:", no punctuation beyond one comma, no lowercase-only fragments, no Title Case On Every Word.

OUTPUT
- Return ONLY the JSON asked for. No markdown fences, no commentary. Escape newlines inside strings as \n.
